Is Polish better than English for prompting AI?

In 2025 headlines said Polish beats English for talking to AI. We traced the claim to its source, then checked it against newer 2025–2026 studies on retrieval, RAG, reasoning and agent-style tasks. Every number here was re-read from the paper's own table.

The headline

“Polish is the best language for prompting AI”

Spider's Web, Polskie Radio and others, Oct–Nov 2025

What the paper measured
88%Polish
83.9%English · 6th of 26

Average accuracy at finding a number hidden in a 64K–128K-token novel, across 7 models from early 2025. A different novel for each language. No significance test.

Short answer: no. With the same model, English is usually equal or better. For 2026 frontier models the gap is small (about 0–3 points). What hurts most is not the language of your data but writing the instructions or forcing the model's thinking in a non-English language.

01 · OneRuler, arXiv 2503.01996

The source: a long-context retrieval benchmark, not a prompting study

OneRuler (Kim, Russell, Karpińska, Iyyer; University of Maryland, Microsoft, UMass Amherst; arXiv 2503.01996, v3 30 Sep 2025) extends the RULER benchmark to 26 languages. Models must find planted “needles” in a book or list the most common words in a long text. Polish came first at 64K and 128K tokens, and that one sentence became the headline.

  • Polish is the best language for prompting and talking to chatbots.
  • AI “loves our grammar”, so we should use Polish more often.
  • Microsoft research proves Polish beats English.
  • Task: find a number in a long novel (NIAH) or aggregate common words (CWE). Not prompting, reasoning or agents.
  • Polish first at 64K/128K with 88% averaged across 7 models; English 6th (83.9%); Chinese 4th from last (62.1%).
  • Each language uses a different book: Little Women (EN, 234K tokens), Noce i dnie vol. 3 (PL, 453K), War and Peace (RU, 1.1M).
  • The instruction language matters: with Korean context, switching instructions to English raises accuracy from 61% to 77% at 128K.
  • After controlling for content length, Slavic languages (RU, UK, PL) stay strong and English is “typically 8th” (Appendix D).

04 · arXiv 2504.17720 · OneRuler §4

The language of the instructions matters more than the language of the content

In an education study, the same Czech, Ukrainian or German content was given to six models twice: once with English task instructions, once with the instructions translated. With English instructions, every model stays near its English baseline. Translated instructions made some models collapse.

Content language
English instructionsTranslated instructionsAll-English baseline

The same pattern appears in OneRuler: with Korean context, switching the instructions to English raised accuracy from 61% to 77% at 128K tokens.

05 · Reasoning and agents

Reasoning models think best in English

Several 2025 studies forced reasoning models to think in the user's language, or let them think in English. Thinking in English scores higher. Agents also do worse and are easier to attack outside English.

06 · arXiv 2604.10799 · 2605.24718

Polish costs 1.3–1.8× more tokens for the same text

Tokenizers are trained mostly on English. The same Polish text is split into more pieces, so it costs more and fills the context window sooner. Polish-specific tokenizers such as APT4 reverse this.

What to do in practice

  1. Write system prompts, tool descriptions and agent instructions in English. Keep Polish data and user messages in Polish, and ask for the answer in Polish.
  2. Let reasoning models think in English. Do not force Polish chain-of-thought.
  3. For RAG over Polish documents, keep the corpus in Polish and use embeddings tested on Polish (PL-MTEB). Evaluate with your own Polish questions.
  4. Budget 1.3–1.8× more tokens for Polish text, both for cost and context size.
  5. With small or open models, test a Polish model (Bielik, PLLuM) against a frontier model on your own task.
  6. Measure on your own evaluation set. A 1–3 point benchmark gap is smaller than the spread between tasks.
08 · Method

How this was researched and checked

  1. Six Claude Haiku research agents searched in parallel: original source, reasoning, retrieval/RAG, native Slavic sources, prompt language and agents, critiques.
  2. Second iteration: Polish reasoning, retrieval, and native-language searches in Polish, Czech, Slovak, Ukrainian, Russian, Bulgarian, Croatian, Serbian and Slovenian.
  3. Iterations 3–5 targeted the remaining gap (Polish RAG and long context), including Polish-language and parallel-benchmark sources.
  4. Five separate verification agents re-checked claims. The coordinator (Claude Opus) then re-read every charted number from the paper's arXiv HTML table.

Verification caught four errors before publication: a reversed “none option” effect, a misread Czech study (the drop came from translating instructions, not content), an unconfirmed MERA Code claim, and several numbers that came only from search snippets.
